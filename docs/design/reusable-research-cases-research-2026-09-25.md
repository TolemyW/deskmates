# Reusable research evaluation cases

Date: 2026-09-25. Primary-source survey only; no dataset was downloaded or case run. Deskmates currently has no general web-research evaluator. Apply the frozen fixture and independent-evidence rules in the [evaluation repair plan](evaluation-repair-plan-2026-09-25.md). A research score must distinguish answer correctness, evidence/citation quality, runtime constraints, and grader uncertainty.

## First candidate: FRAMES, with two exact rows

Google's [FRAMES dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/frames-benchmark) exposes 824 CSV rows with `Prompt`, `Answer`, Wikipedia source-link columns and reasoning labels; its card marks the dataset Apache-2.0. Two accessible starters are `test` **row `Unnamed: 0 = 2`**, a dated arithmetic question about Punxsutawney Phil and the US Capitol (reference answer `87`), and **row `Unnamed: 0 = 13`**, a history question about the independence year of the Belgian colony in *The Poisonwood Bible* (reference `1960`). The row numbers are dataset fields, not invented IDs. Row 2 exercises two-source temporal/numeric reasoning; row 13 is a simpler multi-hop smoke control. Only show `Prompt` to Deskmates; store `Answer` and source-link lists with the grader. An exact normalized number/year answer is a deterministic **answer** predicate. Separately inspect whether the bot cited sources that actually support each hop; the published URL list is a starting reference, not a complete citation-validity oracle. We would build that citation check, a fixed answer parser, fixture reset, and a full-agent runner ourselves. [Dataset card and rows](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/frames-benchmark).

For a repeatable browser campaign, archive the permitted source pages at a declared revision/time and serve them from a controlled local corpus. FRAMES's original questions often say “as of 2024”; live Wikipedia can change and answer disagreement then may be source drift rather than agent failure. A snapshot changes the retrieval environment, so label it **Deskmates FRAMES-derived frozen-corpus protocol**, never a directly comparable FRAMES live-web score. A live-web arm may be useful for product realism but must record fetched URLs/content hashes, failed pages, search provider, and crawl date, and classify obsolete references as an evaluation issue. The [FRAMES paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12941) describes multi-document factual/retrieval/reasoning aims; the downloadable dataset provides questions/answers, not a Deskmates-ready deterministic citation verifier.

## Other options and why they follow later

| Benchmark | Accessible assets and grading | Reuse boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [GAIA](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gaia-benchmark/GAIA/blob/main/README.md) | More than 450 questions across three levels. Public development split has answers; test answers are private. Some cases include attachments, and current Parquet fields include `task_id`, `Question`, `Final answer`, and `file_path`. | Strong future assistant/tool-use sample, but the dataset is gated with an explicit anti-redistribution condition; access and attachment handling come first. Do not publish rows or make a public fixture. The dataset page did not establish a separate license in this survey. |
| [BrowseComp](https://openai.com/index/browsecomp/) | 1,266 difficult, short-answer browsing questions. [Official `simple-evals` code](https://github.com/openai/simple-evals/blob/main/browsecomp_eval.py) downloads encrypted question/answer rows, decrypts them with a canary, and calls a **model grader** for answer equivalence. Its [repository](https://github.com/openai/simple-evals) is MIT licensed and says the reference eval is no longer updated. | Excellent later hard-search stress set, but not a deterministic pass predicate as shipped. Keep decrypted data private, pin the CSV and grader model/version, retain judge outputs, and calibrate ambiguous equivalence against human labels. OpenAI asks users not to publish benchmark examples in plaintext. Do not claim a short-answer pass proves citation quality or useful report writing. |
| [DeepResearch Bench II](https://github.com/imlrz/DeepResearch-Bench-II) | 132 tasks plus `tasks_and_rubrics.jsonl`, report inputs as Markdown/DOCX, an evaluator script and per-dimension aggregation for information recall, analysis and presentation. Its current evaluator calls GPT-5.5 and reports rubric labels with evidence. Code is Apache-2.0; task data has per-source licenses: 129 CC BY 4.0, two CC BY-NC 4.0 (`idx=26,110`), one CC0 (`idx=119`). | Best later **cited-report** track; the judge is model-based and requires a separate API/cost, calibration, and report artifact adapter. Do not treat its score as deterministic or mix it with FRAMES answer accuracy. The rubric/source report must stay outside the agent's workspace. |

## Recommended gate

Start with FRAMES rows 13 and 2 as a controlled multi-hop pilot, then add more rows only after the reference answer and frozen source pages are checked by a human. The full Deskmates agent should search/read through its normal browser/shell under the declared allowlist and fixed time/turn budget; an offline grader should consume only its final answer, saved citations, source snapshots, and trace. Positive controls give the reference answer with valid support; negatives include wrong answer, correct answer with unrelated citation, missing page snapshot, and changed page content. Report the deterministic answer outcome separately from citation review and from budget/tool violations. GAIA, BrowseComp and DeepResearch Bench II become separate campaigns after their gating/judge requirements are met. No portability or success claim is made for Testbox Linux without a live trial.
